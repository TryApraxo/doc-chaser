# Regulation B rules for incomplete applications

A short summary of the Equal Credit Opportunity Act rules (Regulation B, 12 CFR Part 1002) that govern what a creditor must do when a credit application is missing information. Each point names its paragraph; open the source before relying on a detail. ## What counts as an application

- **Application** (1002.2(f)): "an oral or written request for an extension of credit that is made in accordance with procedures used by a creditor for the type of credit requested." It does not include using an existing account or line within a previously established limit.
- **Procedures** means the creditor's actual practices as well as its stated ones. A creditor that says it requires written applications but decides oral requests accepts both (comment 2(f)-2).
- **Inquiry or prequalification becomes an application** when the creditor evaluates information about the consumer, decides to decline, and tells the consumer. It depends on how the creditor responds, not on what the consumer asks (comment 2(f)-3). Explaining loan terms or general policies without saying whether the consumer qualifies is only an inquiry (comment 2(f)-4).
- **Preapproval** requests are applications when the creditor's program issues written commitments after a comprehensive creditworthiness analysis (comment 2(f)-5).

## What counts as a completed application

- **Completed application** (1002.2(f)): one for which the creditor "has received all the information that the creditor regularly obtains and considers in evaluating applications for the amount and type of credit requested", including credit reports, additional information requested from the applicant, and approvals or reports by governmental agencies or others needed to guarantee, insure or provide security for the credit.
- The creditor sets its own information requirements (comment 2(f)-1) but "shall exercise reasonable diligence in obtaining such information" (1002.2(f)). It should request third-party items such as a credit report promptly, and contact the applicant promptly if something is needed from them (comment 2(f)-6).
- Once the creditor has everything it normally considers, the application is complete and the 30-day decision clock runs (comment 9(a)(1)-1).

## The 30-day clocks

| Situation | Deadline | Paragraph |
|---|---|---|
| Completed application: notify of approval, counteroffer or adverse action | 30 days after receiving the completed application | 1002.9(a)(1)(i) |
| Incomplete application, creditor takes adverse action | 30 days after taking adverse action, unless a notice of incompleteness is given under 1002.9(c) | 1002.9(a)(1)(ii) |
| Incomplete application "regarding matters that an applicant can complete" | Within 30 days after receiving it, notify the applicant either of action taken or of the incompleteness | 1002.9(c)(1) |

## The creditor's choices for an incomplete application

1. **Send a notice of incompleteness** (1002.9(c)(1)(ii) and (c)(2)).
2. **Notify of action taken** under 1002.9(a) (1002.9(c)(1)(i)). If the creditor lacks enough data to decide, it may deny and give incompleteness as the reason (comment 9(a)(1)-3).
3. **Decide on what it has.** If the application is missing information but has enough for a decision, the creditor may evaluate and decide. If it denies, it must give the specific reasons; "incomplete application" cannot be the reason (comment 9(a)(1)-4).

## What the notice of incompleteness must contain (1002.9(c)(2))

- It is a **written** notice sent to the applicant
- It **specifies the information needed**
- It **designates a reasonable period of time** for the applicant to provide it
- It **informs the applicant that failure to provide the information requested will result in no further consideration** being given to the application

Form C-6 in Appendix C is the sample notice. Proper use of it "constitutes full compliance with" 1002.9(c)(2) (Appendix C, paragraph 5). Form C-6 carries the creditor's name, address and telephone number, the date, the information needed, a receive-by date, and the no-further-consideration sentence. A creditor may design its own form or use all or part of the sample.

The text read does not define how long a "reasonable period" is.

## Oral requests (1002.9(c)(3))

- A creditor may, at its option, tell the applicant orally what is needed.
- If the application remains incomplete, the creditor must send a notice under 1002.9(c)(1).
- That written notice must go out within the same 30-day period; the oral request does not extend it (comment 9(c)(3)-1).
- If the applicant provides the information, the creditor must act on the application and notify under 1002.9(a) (comment 9(c)(3)-1).

## What happens next

| Applicant's response | Result | Paragraph |
|---|---|---|
| Supplies the information within the designated period | Creditor must take action on the application and notify the applicant under 1002.9(a) | 1002.9(c)(2) |
| Does not respond within the designated period | Creditor has "no further obligation under this section" | 1002.9(c)(2) |
| Supplies the information after the period expires | Creditor may require a new application | Comment 9(c)(2)-1 |

## Other points

- **Preapprovals**: the notice of incompleteness requirement does not apply to preapprovals that are applications under 1002.2(f) (comment 9(c)(1)-1).
- **Multiple applicants**: notice need go to only one, but to the primary applicant where one is readily apparent (1002.9(f)).
- **Small-volume creditors**: a creditor that received no more than 150 applications in the preceding calendar year may satisfy 1002.9 with oral notifications (1002.9(d)).
- **Creditor** (1002.2(l)): a person who, in the ordinary course of business, regularly participates in a credit decision, including setting the terms of the credit.

## Not covered

Adverse action notice contents (1002.9(a)(2) and (b)), business credit (1002.9(a)(3)), record retention, TRID and RESPA timing, HMDA reporting of files closed for incompleteness, FCRA, state law, and investor or agency rules. This file is a summary, not legal advice.
