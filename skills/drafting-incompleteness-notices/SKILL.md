# Drafting notices of incompleteness

Produce the formal last step of document chasing on a credit application: a written notice that names what is missing, sets a date, and states the consequence, in the wording of the regulation's own sample form.

**Important:** This is a draft for the creditor's loan or compliance staff to review and send. It is not legal advice. Whether an application is incomplete, and which notice applies, is the creditor's decision.

## Workflow

1. **Check this is the right notice.** Read `references/reg-b-rules.md`. The notice fits when the application is incomplete regarding matters the applicant can complete. It does not fit when:
   - the creditor has enough information to decide and is denying for another reason (that is an adverse action notice);
   - the creditor chooses to deny for incompleteness (also adverse action);
   - the request is a preapproval that the rule excludes.

   If the facts point to one of these, say so and stop; do not draft.

2. **Collect the facts**, in one message, for whatever is missing:
   - Creditor name, address and telephone number; sender name and title
   - Applicant name and address (the primary applicant, if more than one)
   - The date the incomplete application was received
   - Every item still needed, in words the applicant will recognise
   - Whether the applicant was already asked orally, and when
   - The response date the creditor wants to give

3. **Work out the timing.** The notice must go out within 30 days after the incomplete application was received. Count 30 days from the received date (the rule says days, not business days) and show the result. An earlier oral request does not extend it. If the 30 days have already passed, say so plainly and tell the user to take it to compliance; still draft if asked.

4. **Fill the template** in `assets/notice-of-incompleteness.md`: Variant A for a first written request, Variant B after an oral request. Keep the sample-form sentences as they are. List every missing item on its own line with any look-back period ("pay stubs for the last 30 days"). Use `[TO COMPLETE]` for anything unknown. Leave out the header comment.

5. **Hand over** the full text of the notice in the reply, then the completed timing check from the template, then what happens next (information arrives in time, does not arrive, or arrives late).

## Gotchas

- **The creditor picks the response date.** The rule says "a reasonable period of time" and does not define it. Never choose the date for the user or call a period compliant; ask, and use what they give.
- **Do not soften the consequence sentence.** The statement that the application gets no further consideration is a required element. A friendlier rewrite can drop it.
- **Name every item.** A notice that says "outstanding documents" does not specify the information needed.
- **This is not a denial.** Do not add reasons for denial, credit score disclosures or adverse action wording to this notice.
- **Other clocks keep running.** TRID, HMDA, state law and investor deadlines are outside this skill. Say so when the user asks about closing out a file.
- **Sensitive items travel by a secure channel.** If a missing item is a Social Security number, bank statement or ID, tell the applicant to send it through the lender's portal, never by plain email.
- **Who sends it.** The duty is the creditor's. If the user is a broker, tell them to confirm with the lender who sends the notice.
- **The regulation changes.** The rules were read on 2026-11-11 at https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1002/9/ ; tell the user to confirm the current text.
